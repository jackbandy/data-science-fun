# Data Feminism
D'Ignazio and Klein — https://data-feminism.mitpress.mit.edu

* Introduction: why data science needs feminism
  * power is unequally distributed
  * data never "speak for themselves"
* Examine power
  * who collects data, and for whom
  * how data science reproduces hierarchies
* Challenge power
  * using data to counter dominant structures
  * data activism and civic technology
* Elevate emotion and embodiment
  * limits of "objectivity" as a default
  * how emotion improves data visualization
* Rethink binaries and hierarchies
  * challenging binary classifications
  * intersectionality: race, gender, class, sexuality
* Embrace pluralism
  * local and community knowledge as data
  * participatory data collection
* Consider context
  * data provenance and missing data
  * what gets counted — and what doesn't
* Make labor visible
  * invisible work behind datasets and AI
  * crediting contributors and communities
* Conclusion: toward data feminism in practice
  * from data ethics to data justice
  * practical strategies for equitable data science
