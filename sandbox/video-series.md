### Closest in spirit

* Berkeley Data 8, [Foundations of Data Science (Fall 2020)](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL3juAj0fqNsI4HLvMJFnZDDabxAExG0wv) (40 lectures) — the single best fit for this course: *Computational and Inferential Thinking* is already a source text for Weeks 1, 4, 5, 7, 8, and 11, and these are the lectures that go with it. The Fall 2020 run also ends on a lecture about privacy (2020-12-02), which feeds the privacy thread in `NOTES.md`. `sandbox/data-8-questions/` already mines the assessments; the lectures are the untouched piece.
* [ritvikmath](https://www.youtube.com/@ritvikmath) — the "Data Science Basics" and "Data Science Concepts" series: whiteboard-and-marker, 8 to 15 minutes per concept, covering regularization, bootstrapping, priors, PCA, and SVMs. The closest match to Rho-Signal's cadence for statistics rather than tooling. Weeks 7 through 10.
* [Very Normal](https://www.youtube.com/@very-normal) — a chaptered "Stats From Scratch" series plus one-off explainers on hypothesis tests and false discovery rate. Biostatistics-flavored, pitched at about the level of a sophomore intro course. Weeks 4, 7, and 11.

### Tied to a stretch of weeks

* [StatQuest with Josh Starmer](https://www.youtube.com/@statquest) — dedicated series on linear regression, decision trees, random forests, SVMs, PCA, and ROC/model evaluation, which is Week 8 Day 1 through Week 10 Day 2 almost exactly. Tone is aggressively cheerful; most students take to it, some do not.
* [Luis Serrano Academy](https://www.youtube.com/@SerranoAcademy) — the most geometric treatment of SVMs, kernels, and PCA available, which is what the by-hand worksheet approach wants. Weeks 9 Day 2 and 10 Day 1. Note the channel has drifted heavily toward LLM and agent content; the older machine-learning playlists are the ones to link.

### Checked and not recommended

* [Data School](https://www.youtube.com/@dataschool) (Kevin Markham) — the older pandas and scikit-learn series are excellent and still up, but recent output is entirely LLM tooling. Link individual old videos, not the channel.
* [Mutual Information](https://www.youtube.com/@Mutual_Information) and [Welch Labs](https://www.youtube.com/@welchlabs) — both good, both now pitched well above CS 418 (reinforcement learning, information theory, double descent, LLM architecture). Possible "go deeper" extras, not course material.

### Not found

* No official Polars channel exists under an obvious handle, and a search for consolidated Polars conference-talk playlists turned up nothing. Beyond Rho-Signal, Polars video appears to be scattered across individual PyData and PyCon uploads rather than gathered into a series.
