## Coin Tossing

Used for the "Do You Take the Bet?" slide (Week 1, frequentist vs. Bayesian worldviews).

### `coin-toss.jpg`
A one-euro coin in mid-air above the thumb that flipped it. Photo by ICMA Photos. **CC BY-SA 2.0.**
[Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Coin_Toss_(3635981474).jpg)

### `bradman-allen-toss.jpg`
Don Bradman and G. O. "Gubby" Allen tossing for innings, first Test at Brisbane, 1936–37 Ashes. Unknown author, 1936. **Public domain** (Australian, term expired). Originally [National Library of Australia](http://nla.gov.au/nla.pic-an23448715).
[Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:BradmanAllenToss.jpg)

### `tossing-up-for-the-young-roscius-1804.jpg`
*Theatrical Amusement, or Tossing-up for the Young Roscius!* Two theatre managers toss a coin over the child actor William Betty — "Heads I win — Tails you lose." Published by W. Holland, London, Dec. 5, 1804. **Public domain.** Scan includes the LoC mount and gray scale; crop before use.
[Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Theatrical_amusement_or_Tossing_-_up_for_the_young_Roscius_!_LCCN2006680898.jpg) · [Library of Congress](http://hdl.loc.gov/loc.pnp/cph.3g13954)

### `bartholomew-heads-i-win-tails-you-lose-1912.jpg`
*Heads I Win, Tails You Lose.* Roosevelt flips a coin labeled "Roosevelt Agreement" while Taft and Uncle Sam look on. Charles Lewis "Bart" Bartholomew, *Minneapolis Journal*, Jan. 11, 1912. **Public domain.**
[Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Head_I_Win,_Tails_You_Lose_-_DPLA_-_5ee296db022ad9170cdff087015dd8ac.jpg)

## Other

### `Nightingale-mortality.jpg`
*Diagram of the Causes of Mortality in the Army in the East.* Florence Nightingale, 1858. **Public domain.** See also the portrait in `../portraits/`.
[Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Nightingale-mortality.jpg)

### `sandvig-v-barr-opinion-p1.png`
First page of the memorandum opinion in *Sandvig v. Barr*, No. 16-1368 (JDB) (D.D.C. Mar. 27, 2020), rendered at 150 dpi from the PDF. Academic researchers planned to violate hiring sites' terms of service to audit them for discrimination; the court held the CFAA does not criminalize mere terms-of-service violations on consumer websites. **Public domain** (opinion of a U.S. federal court). Used on the Week 2 "Provenance and Permission" slide.
[PDF, hosted by the ACLU](https://www.aclu.org/sites/default/files/field_document/sandvig_opinion.pdf)

### `boy-who-cried-wolf-milo-winter-1919.jpg`
The shepherd's boy and the wolf, from *The Æsop for Children* (Rand McNally, 1919), illustrated by Milo Winter. **Public domain** (Project Gutenberg etext 19994). Used on the Week 2 Type I / Type II errors slide.
[Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Boy_Who_Cried_Wolf_-_Project_Gutenberg_etext_19994.jpg) · [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/19994)
