# UIC Elevated Walkways

Historical photographs of the elevated concrete walkway system on UIC's east campus — the
"pedestrian highway" that Walter Netsch (Skidmore, Owings & Merrill) designed for the
University of Illinois at Chicago Circle campus in the 1960s, and that was demolished in
the 1990s. All eight images show the walkways at some stage: construction, use, or removal.

## Background

- Netsch put every building entrance on the second floor and connected them with a raised
  granite deck, so students crossed campus above street traffic with shelter underneath.
  The walkway spine fed the Great Court and the Circle Forum amphitheater at the center of
  campus.
- Phase 1 construction started in 1964: precast concrete "butterfly" columns carrying
  granite deck slabs.
- The Great Court, Circle Forum and walkways began coming down **May 10, 1993**, with work
  continuing through the 1990s — framed as opening up a greener campus core and meeting ADA
  requirements, and a major departure from Netsch's original vision. A stone pillar from the
  walkways still stands outside the Architecture and Design Studios.
