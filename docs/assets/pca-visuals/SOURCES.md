# PCA Visuals — Sources

> _Note: this file and the figures were drafted by an LLM coding system and have not yet been reviewed._

All figures are generated by `make_pca_figures.py` (`python3 make_pca_figures.py [name ...]`). Used in the Week 10, Day 1 slides.

- **Data** — `datasets/classification-examples/penguins.csv`, the 342 penguins with all four measurements, each standardized. Allison Horst, Alison Hill, and Kristen Gorman, [palmerpenguins](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3960218) (2020), CC0; data originally from Gorman, Williams, and Fraser, *PLoS ONE* 9(3): e90081 (2014), <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0090081>.
- `pca-penguins-*` — two standardized features, their principal components, and the projection onto PC1. After Jake VanderPlas, *Python Data Science Handbook*, ["In Depth: Principal Component Analysis"](https://jakevdp.github.io/PythonDataScienceHandbook/05.09-principal-component-analysis.html) (text CC BY-NC-ND; figures redrawn with our data).
- `pca-scree` — explained-variance ratio and running total, after PDSH's "Choosing the number of components."
- `pca-loadings` — the PC1 and PC2 weights, after PDSH's "What do the components mean?"
- `pca-species` — all four measurements reduced to PC1 and PC2.

## PCA on PCA (Pete Crow-Armstrong)

- **Data** — `datasets/mlb-center-fielders-2026/center-fielders.csv`, the 40 qualified 2026 MLB center fielders with 11 stats each, standardized. From [Baseball Savant](https://baseballsavant.mlb.com/) (MLB Advanced Media) leaderboards; see that folder's README.
- `pca-cf-scree` — explained variance for all 11 components, drawn like `pca-scree`.
- `pca-cf-loadings` — PC1 and PC2 weights. The titles ("swing big"; "chase and run, don't walk") are our reading of the weights, not something PCA outputs.
- `pca-cf-map-*` — all 40 players on PC1 and PC2; then directions named from the loadings, with four corner players labeled; then Crow-Armstrong in orange. Component signs are fixed so PC1 points toward isolated power and PC2 toward chase rate.
