# Barocas, "Understanding Inscrutability" — Attribution and Provenance

Figures extracted from Solon Barocas's slide deck *Understanding Inscrutability*,

I came to this deck via **Meredith Broussard, *More Than a Glitch*** (MIT Press, 2023; ISBN 9780262047654,
paperback 9780262548328), which cites Solon Barocas and Chandler May in a section about visualization data relationships.
## Source deck

- Solon Barocas (Microsoft Research), **"Understanding Inscrutability"**
- Presented at **"Algorithms and Explanations,"** Information Law Institute, NYU School of Law, **27–28 April 2017** —
  [conference page](https://www.law.nyu.edu/centers/ili/events/algorithms-and-explanations)

## Files

- `barocas-inscrutability.pdf`, `fig-04-*.svg` … `fig-11-*.svg` — the source deck and figures
  extracted from it (PowerPoint charts: glyph outlines plus rasterised surface shading).
- `fig-*-remix.svg` — our own redraws of the same eight ideas, generated by
  `make_inscrutability_figures.py`. Nothing is traced or copied from the originals: the script
  draws each curve and surface from its own function, with its own 3D projection and
  painter's-algorithm renderer, in the repo palette on golden-ratio canvases. Camera
  angles are set per figure to approximate the viewpoint of the matching slide. The
  four-layer-neural-network surface is the output of an actual randomly-initialised four-layer
  ReLU net (seed 10), so its creases are genuine. Re-run with `python3 make_inscrutability_figures.py`.
