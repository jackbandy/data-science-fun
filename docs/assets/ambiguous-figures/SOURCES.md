# Ambiguous figures — media sources

Two classical bistable figures, scanned from the same page of Victor Papanek,
*Design for the Real World: Human Ecology and Social Change* (New York: Pantheon
Books, 1972), p. 159. Cropped to the plate and trimmed; no other retouching.
Used in `docs/slides/week5.qmd` to open the visualization-considerations section.

- **`rubin-goblet-papanek.png`** (619×603) — figure–ground reversal: a white
  goblet on black, or two facing profiles. Papanek's caption reads "Perception
  problem in figure-ground relationship. After Koffka."
  The underlying figure is **Rubin's vase**, from Edgar Rubin,
  *Synsoplevede Figurer* (Copenhagen: Gyldendal, 1915); Kurt Koffka reproduced
  it in *Principles of Gestalt Psychology* (1935). Original public domain by age.
- **`hag-young-girl-papanek.png`** (623×821) — the same lines read as a young
  woman turning away or as an old woman in profile. Papanek's caption reads
  "Old Hag—Young Girl? A classical figure in perception."
  The underlying drawing is **"My Wife and My Mother-in-Law"** by the American
  cartoonist W. E. Hill, published in *Puck*, 6 November 1915, and itself
  adapted from an anonymous German postcard of c. 1888. Public domain by age.

Both underlying images are public domain; the specific renderings here are the
1972 Papanek printing, reproduced for classroom teaching and credited on the
slide. Replace with a Commons scan if a cleaner public-domain copy is wanted.
