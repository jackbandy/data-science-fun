## Vocab figures (`vocab-*.svg`)

Generated by `make_vocab_figures.py` (run it with `docs/slides/.venv/bin/python3 make_vocab_figures.py` from this folder). Intended for the Week 12 vocab slides (Days 1 and 2).

Most figures redraw the running example from Jure Leskovec, Anand Rajaraman & Jeffrey D. Ullman, *Mining of Massive Datasets*, 3rd ed. (Cambridge University Press, 2020), Ch. 10, "Mining Social-Network Graphs" ([doi:10.1017/9781108684163](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108684163)). The chapter text was checked against the authors' PDF (`infolab.stanford.edu/~ullman/mmds/ch10.pdf`, read via the Wayback Machine on 2026-11-09).

- `vocab-graph`, `vocab-neighborhood-v0`…`v3`, `vocab-matrices`, `vocab-communities`: the 7-node graph of Fig. 10.1 ("B is friends with A, C, and D", Example 10.1). The neighborhoods of A match Example 10.26 (profile 3, 4, 7), and the matrices match Figs. 10.13 and 10.14.
- `vocab-overlap`: based on Fig. 10.19 ("The overlap of two communities is denser than the nonoverlapping parts of these communities"); the members and edges are toy data.
- `vocab-directed`, `vocab-closure-v0`/`v1`, `vocab-bipartite-v0`/`v1`: toy data.
- `vocab-graph` adds a self-loop at C that is not in Fig. 10.1, to illustrate the term; it counts 2 toward C's degree.
- `vocab-triadic-v0`/`v1`: the toy friendship graph from worksheet 23, checkpoint 5, illustrating the locality assumption of Sec. 10.1.1 ("if entity A is related to both B and C, then there is a higher probability than average that B and C are related").

The slide quotes come from the chapter summary, Sec. 10.9.
