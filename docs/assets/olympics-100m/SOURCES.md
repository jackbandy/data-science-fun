# Sources — `olympics-100m`

> _Note: this file was drafted with an LLM._

A redrawing of the extrapolation in Tatem et al., "Momentous sprint at the 2156 Olympics?", *Nature* 431:525 (2004), [doi:10.1038/431525a](https://doi.org/10.1038/431525a), for the Week 6 slides. `make_olympics_100m.py` fits one straight line per series to the paper's own data and extends both until they cross (2152.6, so the 2156 Games are the first after it).

## Data

### `data/tatem-2004-table-s1.csv`

Transcribed from the paper's Supplementary Table S1, "Winning times of the men's and women's Olympic 100-metre sprints from 1900" ([MOESM1 .doc](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2F431525a/MediaObjects/41586_2004_BF431525a_MOESM1_ESM.doc)), which cites Rendell, M. (ed.) *The Olympics; Athens to Athens 1896–2004*, 338–340 (Weidenfeld and Nicolson, 2003). The 2000 women's time (10.75) is Marion Jones's, as the paper had it in 2004; that medal was vacated in 2007.

### `data/olympic-100m-2008-2024.csv`

Winning times for the Games after the paper, from the `win_value` field of each edition's English Wikipedia page ("Athletics at the YYYY Summer Olympics – Men's/Women's 100 metres"), retrieved 2026-09-30. The 2024 men's time is rounded from 9.784 to the official 9.79.
