# Sources — `assets/week9`

NOTICE: this file was drafted by an LLM coding system and has not yet been reviewed.

All figures are generated by `make_week9_figures.py` (run `python3 make_week9_figures.py [name ...]`). Most are step sets, `name-v0.svg` … `name-vN.svg`, for the deck's r-stack fragments.

- `knn-steps-v*`, `knn-lazy-v*` — synthetic, hand-placed points. The noisy-nearest-neighbor setup follows Hal Daumé III, *A Course in Machine Learning* (CIML) v0.99, ch. 3, Fig. 3.4.
- `distance-v*` — the worked example a = (3, 4), b = (2, 2) from Gonzalo Bello Lander, CS 418 Lecture 13 (2026), slide 5.
- `ex13-1-v*` — data from Bello, Lecture 13, Exercise 13.1 (slide 8).
- `choose-k-v*`, `tree-grow-v*`, `knn-vs-tree` — the same synthetic 60-point two-class cloud (seed 418), with three labels flipped on purpose. In `choose-k-v*` the cloud is stretched by the golden ratio along x to fill a golden-ratio plot; `knn-steps-v*` and `knn-lazy-v*` stretch their hand-placed points the same way.
- `depth-accuracy-v*`, `k-accuracy-v*` — synthetic, same generator with 10% label noise; 40 draws of 150 training and 1,500 held-out points (the same draws for both).
- `hours-knn-v*`, `hours-threshold-v*`, `split-quiz-hours-v*`, `split-quiz-birthday-v*` — Week 8's 30 synthetic study-hours students (`supervised-two-kinds` in `week8/make_week8_figures.py`, seed 418: hours uniform on 0–10, score 45 + 5 × hours + noise, pass at 70 or more; 20 pass, 10 fail), plus a random day of month born (`seed=182`). The seed was picked from 300 so that "born on or before the 15th?" has zero information gain (10 pass / 5 fail on each side) and k-NN visibly follows birthdays (38% of the plotted plane flips prediction once day is added; the median over the 300 seeds is 28%). A noise feature on the same scale as hours does not do this: with birth month (1–12) or a 0–9 ID digit, median leave-one-out k-NN accuracy over 300 seeds stays at 87%, the same as hours alone. The second `hours-knn` frame is drawn at equal scale, with days along x. The 6.11-hour threshold is scikit-learn's depth-1 entropy tree. The threshold-tree framing is based on CIML ch. 3, §3.3; no figure is copied.
- `split-quiz-seat-v*`, `split-quiz-practice-v*` — invented counts for the same 20 pass / 10 fail students: "Sits on the left side of the room?" (11/5 and 9/5, information gain 0.002) and "Took the practice exam?" (2/9 and 18/1, information gain 0.479).
- `irrelevant-v*` — synthetic, hand-placed points.
- `tax-tree-v*`, `tax-path-*` — the tax-cheating tree from Bello, CS 418 Lecture 12 (2026), slides 8–11, redrawn.
- `majority-vote-v*` — invented counts: 20 fans (9 Cubs, 11 White Sox) split on "Lives south of Madison Street?" into 7 Cubs / 3 Sox and 2 Cubs / 8 Sox, scoring 15/20. The scoring rule is CIML ch. 1, §1.3 (whose example is 20 course ratings and "Is this a Systems course?", scoring 18/20).
- `split-entropy-v*` — the same invented Cubs / White Sox counts as `majority-vote-v*`, scored with entropy (parent 0.993, children 0.881 and 0.722, information gain 0.191).
- `majority-tie-v*` — counts and entropies from Elena Zheleva, CS 418 lecture slides, Feb. 19, 2026 (using materials by Marine Carpuat).
- `survey-*` — the deck's running example, a synthetic version of worksheet checkpoint 1 (did each student take each of 12 CS courses?), predicting CS 412 from the other 11. `make_survey` in the script is the same generator as the code demo in `week9.qmd`: hidden traits (senior, into AI, into games) set each course's odds; CS 251 is 1 for everyone because it is the CS 418 prerequisite. The 20 classmates and Alice are rows 0–19 and 33 of `make_survey(60, seed=18981)`, a seed and row picked by search so that every teaching point appears (CS 424 and CS 411 tie at 18/20 by majority vote but not by entropy; Jaccard and SMC disagree on Alice's nearest neighbor; k = 1 and k = 3 disagree). `survey-choose-k-v*` and `survey-depth-accuracy-v*` use the demo's 1,000 students (`seed=418`), split 600 / 200 / 200. Birth months on `survey-month-v*` are random (`seed=0`). Course numbers and titles match the UIC catalog.
- `ex12-4-v*`, `entropy-curve-v*` — Bello, Lecture 12, Exercise 12.4 and slides 15–18 (IG 0.029 vs. 0.278); the entropy curve is plain math. Reading information gain off the curve (parent point, children joined by a straight line, their average on that line) follows the annotated entropy plot in Sebastian Raschka, "Why are we growing decision trees via entropy instead of the classification error?", Machine Learning FAQ, https://sebastianraschka.com/faq/docs/decisiontree-error-vs-entropy.html; no figure is copied.
- `svm-lines-v*` — 14 Adélie and 14 Gentoo penguins drawn at random (`seed=0`, the first seed whose sample leaves a visible gap) from Allison Horst, Alison Hill, and Kristen Gorman, palmerpenguins (2020), via `datasets/classification-examples/penguins.csv`; bill length and depth standardized over all 342 measured penguins and drawn at equal scale, with millimeter tick labels. Line B is scikit-learn `SVC(kernel="linear", C=1e6)` (a hard margin); A is B shifted toward the Gentoo side until 40% of its gap is left, and C is B rotated about its center as far as it can go while still separating the species and keeping 40% of the gap. The "which line?" setup follows Bello, CS 418 Lecture 13 (2026), Exercise 13.3, and Jake VanderPlas, *Python Data Science Handbook*, "In-Depth: Support Vector Machines"; no figure is copied.
- `svm-soft-v*` — all 274 Adélie and Gentoo penguins (the Week 8 example), same standardized axes, linear SVC with C = 100 and C = 0.01.
- `kernel-lift-v*` — invented 1D points (+ in the middle, − on both ends) lifted to (x, x²), based on Bello, Lecture 13, slide 26 ("Nonlinear SVM").
- `kernel-penguins-v*` — all 342 measured penguins, Chinstrap (68) against Adélie and Gentoo, same standardized axes; linear SVC, then RBF SVC with scikit-learn defaults.
- `kernel-shape-{a..e}-v*` — synthetic blobs, rings, bands, XOR corners, and moons (`seed=418` and fixed `random_state`s), one shape per set, the five shape kinds of the PrairieLearn question `kernel-needed`; the script asserts that exactly the blobs and bands are linearly separable.
- `svm-gamma-v*` — the Chinstrap-vs.-rest penguins, split half for training and half held out (stratified, `random_state=0`), RBF SVC with C = 1 and γ = 0.05, 1, 50.
- `similarity-venn` — the Jaccard and simple matching coefficient formulas from Bello, CS 418 Lecture 13 (2026), slide 6, drawn as fractions of Venn diagrams: circles for the attributes where a = 1 and where b = 1, inside a golden-ratio rectangle of all attributes. Plain math; no data.
- `movie-path-loved`, `movie-path-ok`, `movie-path-horrible` — the root-to-leaf path of three example reviews through the Python Yard demo's decision tree (`docs/yard/content/movie-reviews.ipynb`). The script runs the notebook's own word-list and `make_features` cells on `datasets/movie-reviews/reviews.csv` and refits the tree with the notebook's settings (80/20 split, `max_depth=5`, `min_samples_leaf=25`, `random_state=418`), so the figures follow any edit to the word lists.
- `kernel-types` — the Chinstrap-vs.-rest penguins (as in `kernel-penguins-v*`) fit with each of scikit-learn's four built-in `SVC` kernels: linear, polynomial (`degree=3`, `coef0=1`, so the kernel is (γ u·v + 1)³ with scikit-learn's default γ), RBF, and sigmoid, all other settings default; each title gives training accuracy.
